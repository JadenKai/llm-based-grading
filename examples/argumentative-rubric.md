# Argumentative Essay Rubric (25 points)

Use this rubric to score a single-author argumentative essay of roughly 500–900 words.

| Criterion | Points | Exemplary (full marks) | Proficient | Developing | Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Claim & Thesis** | 5 | A precise, arguable thesis appears in the introduction and previews the main lines of reasoning. | Clear, arguable thesis; preview of reasons is partial. | Thesis is present but vague, overly broad, or appears late. | No identifiable thesis, or the thesis is a statement of fact. |
| **Evidence & Accuracy** | 6 | Specific, relevant and accurate evidence (statistics, examples, cases) supports every reason. | Evidence supports most reasons; minor vagueness. | Evidence is general, thin, or partly irrelevant; one factual error. | Little or no evidence, or several factual errors. |
| **Reasoning & Analysis** | 5 | Explains *how* each piece of evidence proves the claim; reasoning is logical and insightful. | Analysis is present for most evidence but sometimes summary-like. | Evidence is mostly listed rather than explained. | Claims are asserted without support or explanation. |
| **Counterclaim & Rebuttal** | 3 | Fairly states a serious opposing view and rebuts it convincingly. | Acknowledges a counterclaim; rebuttal is brief. | Counterclaim is a straw man or the rebuttal is missing. | No counterclaim. |
| **Organization & Coherence** | 3 | Every paragraph serves the thesis; logical order; clear topic sentences and transitions. | Mostly focused and ordered; one weak transition. | One paragraph is off-topic or out of place. | Disorganized; frequent digressions. |
| **Language & Conventions** | 3 | Precise, formal style; essentially free of grammar, usage and spelling errors. | A few minor errors that do not distract. | Repeated errors (agreement, homophones, articles) that distract. | Errors frequently obscure meaning. |

## Grading notes

- Personal anecdotes that do not support the thesis count against **Organization & Coherence**, not just **Reasoning**.
- A factual inaccuracy lowers **Evidence & Accuracy** even if the surrounding argument is strong.
- Do not deduct twice for the same grammar error; judge the overall pattern under **Language & Conventions**.
- Scores of 23–25 indicate an A-range essay, 20–22 B-range, 17–19 C-range.
